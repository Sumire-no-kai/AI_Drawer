# Product

## Register

product

## Users

AI Drawer is for Windows users who regularly use one or more browser-based AI services and want to keep several AI work contexts available without restoring a full browser session. Core users may keep different providers open at the same time or maintain multiple independent work contexts within the same provider. They value keyboard access, fast switching, persistent sign-in, and a desktop utility that stays out of the way until needed.

## Product Purpose

AI Drawer is a lightweight, native desktop entry point for compatible official AI web applications. A global shortcut opens a focused workspace where users can start, resume, and switch between provider-managed conversations. AI Drawer preserves provider sessions but does not duplicate, inspect, or locally cache conversation content. Success means that users can return to the right AI work context faster than restoring browser tabs, while resource use remains bounded and provider limitations remain honest.

## Brand Personality

Lightweight, restrained, trustworthy. AI Drawer should feel modern and calm without becoming decorative, sterile, or anonymous. Its voice is direct and plain-spoken. It behaves like a dependable Windows utility: quick to appear, easy to understand, and quiet when not in use.

## Anti-references

- A general-purpose browser clone with an address bar, bookmarks, and unbounded tabs.
- A colorful marketplace grid that presents providers as collectible AI products.
- Generic AI branding built from neon gradients, glowing orbs, glass cards, or robot imagery.
- A dense enterprise dashboard with analytics, usage charts, or account-management chrome unrelated to the user's current task.
- An interface that implies AI Drawer owns provider accounts, subscriptions, conversations, or payment flows.

## Design Principles

1. **Open the work, not the app.** The shortcut should return users to a useful AI context with minimal intermediate navigation.
2. **Support many contexts without becoming a browser.** Multiple providers and same-provider work contexts are first-class, while live WebViews and navigation scope remain deliberately bounded.
3. **Keep native controls quiet and dependable.** The shell explains state, recovery, permissions, and limitations, then yields most of the window to the provider experience.
4. **Protect privacy through structural restraint.** Do not inspect or duplicate provider conversation content, credentials, cookies, tokens, DOM, or payment data.
5. **Make boundaries visible before they matter.** Provider compatibility, external navigation, purchases, resets, and recovery actions must explain ownership and consequences in plain language.
6. **Stay fast by design.** Native UI appears before provider content, inactive work contexts do not create unbounded live renderers, and every loading or recovery state remains responsive.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Target WCAG 2.2 AA for native product surfaces.
- Support complete keyboard navigation with visible, predictable focus.
- Remain usable at 200% display scaling without clipped controls or hidden actions.
- Respect Windows high-contrast themes and reduced-motion preferences.
- Maintain readable light and dark themes.
- Do not rely on color alone for provider state, compatibility, warnings, or errors.
- Keep purchase and privacy disclosures understandable without technical knowledge.
